## Private risk read
- Demand is the biggest unknown. At the low end of our estimate, Google spends only a few hundred dollars a month and consults are rare. Say so on the call before a competitor quotes bigger numbers; the day 30 impression share report settles it.
- CPC may run higher than modeled. Agency pages claim $15 to $50 clicks for orthopedic terms; if true, contacts at $3,000 fall by about half. The yellow CPC cells show the effect live.
- TCO is the gate. It has a chief marketing officer and an agency of record. If physician campaigns must run through them, or are barred, the engagement changes shape. Ask at the call, not after signature.
- Knee replacement is 40% of the pilot allocation and unverified for him; if he does few, that budget moves to hip replacement and hip preservation, as the proposal says.
- The RFP's measurement standard depends on the practice: five yes or no codes per new-patient contact, reviewed monthly. Without that, cost per consult cannot be reported. The 30% to 60% contact-to-qualified rate has no orthopedic benchmark.
- Unusual or costly to honor: the exclusivity request; six procedures against an entry budget (concentration may disappoint if they expected all six on day one); HIPAA-grade vendors (a call tracking plan with a BAA and a BAA-covered form tool, typically a few hundred dollars a month, for the practice to carry or you to pass through).
- Robotic and navigated wording: Minnesota's misleading-advertising statute and Google's policy both apply if an ad claims a technology he does not use; the ads hold those lines until the practice confirms the system and facility.
