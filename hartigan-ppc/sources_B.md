## Assumptions

- Assumption: B50 | The CSV's searches_low and searches_high are monthly searches for the target geography defined as the Minneapolis-St. Paul metro, which contains the three priority suburbs (the union of suburbs plus metro equals the metro). Suburb-only and Minnesota-only views are derived in the summary using B51 to B53. | The RFP defines the target geography as the priority suburbs plus the broader Twin Cities; a single metro column avoids double counting. | A Keyword Planner export run with the three cities and the DMA as separate locations would replace these derived views.
- Assumption: B51 | National to metro multiplier: low 1.1%, high 1.4%. | Low is the MSA share of the US population: 3,760,000 (B17) / 341,784,857 (B15) = 1.10%. High approximates the larger DMA (1,886,680 TV households (B22) times about 2.4 persons per household is about 4.5 million, or 1.32% of the US) plus a small allowance for Minnesota's above-average joint replacement interest (B12, B25), rounded to 1.4%. | Keyword Planner location data for the DMA; Google Trends metro index values (B23, not reached) showing the Twin Cities above or below the national average for these terms.
- Assumption: B52 | National to priority-suburbs multiplier: low 0.06%, high 0.18%. | Low is the residents of the three cities: 78,942 (B19) + 73,893 (B20) + 53,261 (B21) = 206,096, or 0.060% of the US. High reflects a clinic catchment of about 600,000 people (about three times the resident total), because people search from home, work and nearby cities: the Edina clinic draws Bloomington, Richfield, Eden Prairie, Minnetonka, St. Louis Park, Hopkins and southwest Minneapolis; the Maple Grove clinic draws Plymouth, Brooklyn Park, Champlin, Osseo, Rogers, Medina, Golden Valley, New Hope and Crystal; the catchment is still well under half of Hennepin County (B18). | Keyword Planner run with the three cities plus a 10-mile radius around each clinic; the practice's own patient ZIP code distribution.
- Assumption: B53 | National to Minnesota multiplier (hip preservation cluster only): low 1.7%, high 2.0%. | Low is the population share: 5,830,405 (B16) / 341,784,857 (B15) = 1.71%. High adds an allowance for above-average hip search interest in Minnesota (B12) and for patients who travel within the state for a subspecialist. | Keyword Planner run for Minnesota; the practice's share of out-of-metro hip arthroscopy patients.
- Assumption: B54 | Band for third-party anchored national figures: low 0.7x and high 1.3x the published figure. | Keyword tools differ from each other and from Keyword Planner by roughly this much, and the B8 figure is several years old. | A current Keyword Planner 12-month average for the same keyword.
- Assumption: B55 | Band for modeled national figures: the modeled point times 0.6 (low) and 1.5 (high), rounded to two significant figures; local figures are rounded to two significant figures and never shown below 1. | Modeled figures deserve a wider band than anchored ones. | Keyword Planner data replaces the band with an actual average and a monthly trend.
- Assumption: B56 | Head-term ratios: "hip replacement" is 4x to 9x "hip replacement surgery" (B8 anchor 9,900, giving 40,000 to 90,000); knee terms are 1.29x the matching hip terms (B9: 71.47 / 55.38); "total" and "anterior" variants are about 0.1x the head term; arthritis treatment terms are about 0.05x to 0.1x the head term; cost terms about 0.05x to 0.1x; recovery-time terms about 0.1x. | Head terms absorb close variants and dominate procedure interest; the ratios are typical of Keyword Planner groupings for procedure families and are stated so they can be corrected. | Any Keyword Planner export.
- Assumption: B57 | Surgeon-seeking share: "[procedure] surgeon" is 3% to 8% of the head procedure term; "near me" variants are 0.8x to 1.0x the surgeon term (B7 shows "near me" terms rivaling plain terms for orthopedics); "doctor" variants are about 0.3x the surgeon term; "best" variants about 0.4x; "[joint] pain doctor" and "specialist near me" terms are modeled at 1,000 to 5,000 nationally; "sports medicine doctor near me" and "shoulder surgeon near me" are set from the B7 near-me scale at 2,000 to 20,000 nationally. | Surgeon-seeking is a small slice of total procedure interest, and local intent is strong for provider searches. | Keyword Planner export.
- Assumption: B58 | Geo-modified keywords (a city or state name in the query) are inherently local, so their full volume counts in every geography (suburbs, metro, Minnesota). Modeled at 5 to 20 per month for a suburb-modified term, 10 to 60 for a Minneapolis-modified hip or knee term, 5 to 30 for a Minneapolis-modified sports or hip arthroscopy term, and 5 to 20 for "hip preservation Minnesota". These sit below the metro slice of the matching plain term, which is the expected relationship. | Keyword Planner often reports such terms as 10 or fewer per month; they earn their place through message match and low CPC rather than volume. | Keyword Planner export; search term reports after launch.
- Assumption: B59 | Sports and hip preservation cluster nationals are modeled by analogy with no per-keyword anchor: condition head terms (torn meniscus 60,000 to 140,000; ACL tear 40,000 to 90,000; rotator cuff tear 30,000 to 70,000; hip labral tear 15,000 to 35,000; hip impingement 12,000 to 30,000) are set relative to the hip replacement head term using the relative-interest signals in B13 and B14; procedure terms (ACL surgery, rotator cuff surgery 15,000 to 35,000; meniscus surgery 8,000 to 20,000; hip arthroscopy 5,000 to 12,000) at roughly 0.2x to 0.4x their condition term; surgeon-seeking terms in these clusters at 1% to 3% of the procedure term, lower than joint replacement, because these patients are younger, often start with symptom research, and frequently arrive by referral or through physical therapy. This is the thinnest part of the file. | No third-party figure for any of these keywords was reachable without a login. | Keyword Planner export (15 minutes) would replace every number in these clusters.
- Assumption: B60 | CPC uplift over national category averages: Tier 1 surgeon-seeking terms 1.5x to 4x the physicians and surgeons category CPC of $4.76 to $5.00 (B1, B2), giving $7 to $20 ($8 to $22 for near-me variants); Tier 2 procedure and technology terms 1.2x to 3x, giving $6 to $16; Tier 3 condition research 0.6x to 1.6x, giving $2 to $8; hip preservation and sports cluster surgeon terms $5 to $18; geo-modified long-tail $5 to $16. | Category averages blend every physician ad type. Surgical procedure terms with a case value in the tens of thousands of dollars attract hospital systems, large orthopedic groups and device-maker patient marketing; the Twin Cities is a top-20 market with several large orthopedic competitors; the healthcare average is already $5.64 (B3) and an orthopedics figure of $6.15 circulates (B5). Agency claims of $15 to $50 (B6) are treated as the upper tail, not the expectation. | Keyword Planner top-of-page bid ranges for the DMA; the first 30 days of auction data.
- Assumption: B61 | Competition labels: high for joint replacement Tier 1 and Tier 2 terms and for generic near-me provider terms; medium for sports medicine, hip preservation and Tier 3 terms; low for thin geo-modified long-tail and clinical terms. | Reflects who bids: hospital systems and device makers on joint replacement, subspecialists on hip preservation, fewer advertisers on clinical terms. | Keyword Planner competition index; Auction Insights after launch.
- Assumption: B62 | No metro-level relative-interest adjustment from Google Trends was applied beyond the small uplift in B51 and B53. | Google Trends was not reachable (B23). | A Trends export by metro for the top 10 terms.
- Assumption: B63 | Demand ceiling arithmetic: for non-geo rows, suburbs = metro low x (0.06 / 1.1) and metro high x (0.18 / 1.4); Minnesota = metro low x (1.7 / 1.1) and metro high x (2.0 / 1.4); geo-modified rows are counted as-is in every view; totals are rounded to two significant figures after summing. Ceilings sum Tier 1 and Tier 2 rows only. | The multipliers are linear, so deriving from the metro column is identical to deriving from the national figures in the notes. | Nothing; this is the computation rule for the summary table.
- Assumption: B64 | A 15-minute Keyword Planner export (locations: Plymouth, Maple Grove, Edina, Minneapolis-St. Paul DMA, Minnesota; 12-month averages; top-of-page bid low and high) would replace B54 to B60 with observed values and turn every low-confidence row into medium or high confidence. | Keyword Planner is the primary source in the run's source priority and is unavailable only because no logged-in account exists in this environment. | Access to any Google Ads account, even a new one with no spend.
- Assumption: B65 | Third-party figures are 2024 or older (B7, B8) and search behavior has shifted since; Tier 3 research terms lose clicks to AI Overviews while Tier 1 provider-intent terms do not (B24), so Tier 3 volumes overstate clickable demand more than Tier 1 volumes do. | Stated so the budget model discounts Tier 3 rather than treating all tiers alike. | Keyword Planner impression forecasts by tier.
