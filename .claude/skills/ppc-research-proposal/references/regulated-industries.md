# Regulated industries: conditional, proportionate

Apply this file only when the prospect's industry is regulated (healthcare, legal, financial, insurance, education, housing, alcohol, cannabis, and similar). A non-regulated proposal carries none of it. Even in a regulated vertical, the first proposal carries two or three sentences; the architecture belongs to Stage 2.

## What the first proposal needs
- Any Google Ads policy that changes whether or how the ads can run (category certifications, restricted claims, sensitive-category targeting limits). Verify the current policy page before stating it.
- The tracking posture in one or two sentences: sensitive or protected information stays out of ad platforms; calls and forms are counted without their contents; vendors that touch protected data sign the vertical's data agreement; the prospect's compliance owner and counsel confirm the setup.
- Advertising-claims rules that shape copy (no outcome promises, testimonial rules, professional-conduct advertising rules).
- A line that these are flags for the prospect's compliance owner and counsel, not legal advice.

## What waits for Stage 2
Vendor selection and data agreements (BAAs), call-routing and number-insertion design, analytics configuration, offline-conversion and enhanced-conversion decisions, consent and restricted-data-processing settings, statutory citations, data-flow tables, testimonial authorizations, trademark reviews of specific device or product names.

## Healthcare (HIPAA covered entities)
First proposal: no health-condition audiences or remarketing; no PHI to Google; calls and forms tracked through vendors that sign BAAs, counted without contents; the practice's compliance team and counsel review the setup; outcomes (consults, procedures) reported by the practice in aggregate. Google Ads healthcare policy: in-person clinical services generally need no certification; no cure or outcome claims. Stage 2 items: HHS OCR tracking-technology guidance and its litigation status, state consumer-health and health-records laws, medical-board advertising rules, FTC endorsement rules, device brand names in copy (only for systems the practice uses), vendor BAAs (call tracking, forms, CRM), Google's lack of a BAA for Analytics and Ads. Verify citations against primary sources; run prompts have carried wrong statute chapters.

## Legal
First proposal: state bar advertising rules (no misleading statements, no unjustified expectations, "specialist" claims only where certified, required labels), personal-hardship categories excluded from audience targeting, inquiries kept confidential. Stage 2: the specific rules of professional conduct, disclaimers, intake confidentiality, call-recording consent.

## Financial services and insurance
First proposal: Google's financial products certifications or verification where applicable; no rate or outcome claims without disclosures; financial-hardship categories excluded from targeting. Stage 2: regulator-specific disclosure rules, GLBA safeguards for lead data.

## Housing, employment, credit
Google's policy restricts location, demographic, and interest targeting for these categories in the US; note it and design targeting accordingly.

## Everyone
FTC Endorsement Guides and the consumer reviews rule for any testimonial or rating claim; call-recording consent by state; competitor names never in ad text; product brand names only when the prospect actually offers them.
