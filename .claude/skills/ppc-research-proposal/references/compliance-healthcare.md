# Compliance module: healthcare (US)

Use for physicians, surgical practices, dental, physical therapy, mental health, and any HIPAA covered entity. Agent C verifies each item as of the run date with citations; the orchestrator holds the defaults. Nothing here is legal advice. Statute citations change and run prompts have carried wrong ones (a 2026 run's prompt cited "chapter 325O" for the Minnesota Consumer Data Privacy Act; the Revisor lists it at Minn. Stat. 325M.10 to 325M.21), so verify every citation against the primary source before it reaches a deliverable.

## Verification list
1. Google Ads Healthcare and medicines policy for the category: in-person surgical and clinical services generally need no certification (certifications cover pharmacies, addiction services, telehealth, and similar); no improbable-outcome or cure claims. Check the policy's latest update notes.
2. Google personalized advertising: health (including invasive procedures) is a sensitive interest category, so no remarketing lists, Customer Match, custom segments, or health-based audiences; check the current exceptions (for example ads to clinicians).
3. Google customer data policies: no uploading conversion information related to sensitive categories, including purchases of medical services; Enhanced Conversions (hashed identifiers) therefore off; offline conversion import only as a minimum-data option with written approval.
4. Google Analytics and Google Ads: Google offers no BAA and says regulated entities must not expose PHI; default to no GA4 on pages that collect health information unless the privacy officer approves a stripped configuration.
5. Google call reporting and recording: routes calls through Google numbers and can record; keep it off and use a BAA-signed vendor's static number for call assets.
6. HHS Office for Civil Rights guidance on online tracking technologies (December 2022 bulletin, March 2024 update) and the June 20, 2024 ruling in American Hospital Association v. Becerra (N.D. Texas) that vacated the "IP address plus unauthenticated health page" portion; OCR withdrew its appeal in August 2024. Still in effect: BAAs for any tool receiving PHI and no tracking that discloses PHI on authenticated or scheduling pages. Check for newer OCR guidance, FTC actions (express consent before health data reaches ad platforms), and state class actions naming local systems.
7. State privacy law: the state's consumer data privacy act (thresholds, sensitive-data consent, HIPAA exemptions, cure period, enforcement), any consumer health data law (Washington's My Health My Data Act as the model), and the state health records act (consent to release). Verify codified sections.
8. Physician advertising and testimonials: the state medical practice act's grounds for discipline (false, deceptive, or misleading advertising; superiority claims), the licensing board's rules if any, FTC Endorsement Guides (2023 revision), and the FTC Consumer Reviews and Testimonials Rule (effective October 21, 2024). HIPAA requires written authorization to use PHI (including a patient's story) in marketing.
9. Trademarks in ad copy: Google restricts trademarks in ad text on owner complaint, not as keywords in the US; device and system brand names (Mako by Stryker, ROSA by Zimmer Biomet, VELYS by DePuy Synthes, CORI by Smith and Nephew, OrthAlign, and the like) only for systems the practice actually uses, confirmed at kickoff; the group's name only with written approval; no competitor names in ad text.
10. Consent mode and restricted data processing: consent signals apply to EEA, UK, and Swiss traffic; restricted data processing is counsel's call for US state laws.
11. Google's current guidance on conversion volume for automated bidding (Target CPA evaluated over 30 days with at least 30 conversions is the long-standing figure; confirm the current Help page) so bidding can be planned by phase.

## Vendor categories that sign BAAs (verify current terms)
Call tracking: CallRail (Healthcare plan), CallTrackingMetrics, Invoca, WhatConverts; Marchex has disclaimed business-associate status. Forms: Jotform (HIPAA plan), Formstack; Gravity Forms does not; Typeform needs a custom agreement. CRM: HubSpot only on Enterprise with its healthcare terms, Salesforce Health Cloud. Recommend requirements and categories in the proposal, not one vendor.

## Defaults in the proposal
No health audiences; no PHI to Google; DNI from a BAA vendor; static vendor number for call assets with Google call reporting off; BAA-covered form sending an event only; no Google lead form assets; GA4 only in a stripped, approved configuration; offline import off until approved in writing; outcomes in aggregate; a BAA between the prospect and the sender if the sender touches recordings or form contents; the prospect's compliance team and counsel approve the setup and all copy; testimonials only with written authorization and a release, labeled as an individual experience.
